01 / Why now

The supply chain is a lot of small shops.

Primes build the aircraft. Many of the parts come from small machine shops working to customer drawings. When a drawing changes, the shop has to catch it before cutting metal. When a part ships, someone has to show how it was made. In a lot of small shops, both still run on paper and memory, and three recent changes make that harder to get away with.

  • 10 Nov 2026

    CMMC Phase 2

    Defense contracts can now require a third-party Level 2 cybersecurity certification at award.[1] Shops that hold controlled drawings have to show they protect them.[2]

  • 10 Jul 2025

    Drones as consumables

    The Pentagon’s “Unleashing U.S. Military Drone Dominance” memo treats small drones as consumables to be bought and fielded at scale, not kept for years.[3] That means more parts, made faster, by more suppliers.

  • FY2020 NDAA §848

    Built here

    The Defense Department can’t buy drones, or key drone parts, made in China,[4] and the rest of the federal government followed in the FY2024 NDAA.[5] Domestic parts need domestic suppliers who can document their work.
