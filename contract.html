A hypothetical contract for a small UAV that tows a sensor. Two student startups from Hofstra’s AIAA branch bid on it. Faculty decide.

Exercise onlyHAIAA-27-01 is a student exercise. It isn’t a government solicitation, no money changes hands, and the schedule below is still a draft.

Why a contract

Hofstra’s AIAA branch sent a proposal to the 2026–27 AIAA Design/Build/Fly competition and wasn’t selected to build. Instead of waiting a year, the branch is running the mission as a contract competition of its own.

Most student design projects end at a grade. Defense work ends at an award decision, and the bidder who loses gets a debrief on why. Practicing that, losing included, is the point.

The requirement is the same DBF 2026–27 mission the branch proposed for.[1] A few requirements are added that a real buyer would ask for: a production lot, a record of how each unit was built, and where the parts come from.

Statement of work

R-1 to R-5 paraphrase the draft DBF rules; read the rules for the exact wording. Rows marked in red are added for this exercise.

ReqRequirementFrom
R-1VehicleA UAV with a wingspan of 6 feet or less.DBF §3.1
R-2Towed sensorAt least 6 inches long, carried inside the aircraft until deployed, and stable (not spinning) while deployed and recovered.DBF §3.1.1
R-3LightsForward, center and aft lights with three modes (off, solid, flashing), commanded from the aircraft through the tow line. The sensor carries its own battery.DBF §3.1.1
R-4Deploy and recoverIn flight, with a mechanism inside the aircraft, to a tow length of at least 1.5 times the wingspan.DBF §3.1.2
R-5ShippingThe sensor travels in a rectangular container that protects it from drop shock.DBF §3.1.3
R-6Build recordEvery unit ships with the drawing revisions it was built to and a log of who built and inspected it.Exercise
R-7SourcingList where every flight-critical part comes from. No drone parts from covered foreign countries.Exercise, after §848[3] and Blue UAS[4]
R-8Production lotPrice and schedule for a lot of units. The judges set the quantity.Exercise, after the drone memo[5]

How the winner is picked

Best value, not lowest price. Judges compare the bids on four factors, listed from most to least important. The three non-price factors together count for more than price.[2]

  1. Technical approach. Does the design meet R-1 to R-5, and how well, shown by analysis, test or flight.
  2. Production and quality. Can the bidder build the lot, and prove how each unit was built and where its parts came from (R-6, R-7).
  3. Schedule. A plan with milestones the judges believe.
  4. Price. Parts and labor for the lot (R-8).

Ten weeks, solicitation to award.

    Solicitation out

    Questions

    Proposals due

    Technical volume, production plan, schedule and price.

    Orals

    Each bidder presents and takes questions from the judges.

    Award and debrief

    Judges announce the winner and debrief both teams.
